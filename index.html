PA-Marine-Model

A weekly early-warning forecast for toxic Dinophysis blooms in Irish shellfish waters

Every Sunday, for each Marine Institute monitoring station sampled that week, PA-Marine-Model estimates the chance that Dinophysis — the plankton behind diarrhetic shellfish poisoning (DSP) — reaches ≥100 cells per litre in the next two weeks. Built on open monitoring data, tested against honest baselines, with every null result published.

By Garry Lohan · Lecturer, ATU Galway

What we can say

  • The Sunday forecast ranks the coming fortnight’s bloom weeks clearly better than the seasonal norm: PR-AUC 0.31 vs 0.23 nationally (2022–26), and 0.34 vs 0.20 in the South-West.
  • The skill comes from recent counts at and near each site. A simple logistic model comes within about 0.01.
  • No environmental input — sea temperature, heatwaves, wind, chlorophyll, currents, rivers — added fair skill.
  • The same recipe works in France, beating the French seasonal norm by 0.10–0.13.

What we can’t say (yet)

  • It does not catch more shellfish closures than MI’s own free ≥100 cells/L rule; its edge is far fewer false alarms.
  • About half the gain is telling busy years from quiet ones, not week-by-week timing.
  • Marine heatwaves are getting more frequent (about +29 days per decade), but we found no detectable effect on plankton or toxic blooms.
  • Skill so far is from hindcasts. Genuine scoring starts 25 Oct 2026; 2027 is the confirmatory season.

About

PA-Marine-Model is a research prototype by Garry Lohan, lecturer at Atlantic Technological University (ATU) Galway, built for Ocean Hackathon Cork 2026. It is not an official Marine Institute product or advice; shellfish safety is decided by official monitoring.

Contains Marine Institute data (open HAB monitoring via ERDDAP), E.U. Copernicus Marine Service information and other open data; this site shows national, regional and yearly aggregates only. Data sources · Credits
