Conas í a léamhHow to read the map

Each dot is a Marine Institute shellfish monitoring site. Its colour shows the chance that Dinophysis, a plankton that can make shellfish unsafe to eat, reaches 100 cells per litre at that site over the next two weeks. A new outlook is issued every week.

  • Lowunder 5%
  • Watch5–15%
  • Elevated15–30%
  • High30% or more
  • Paler, dashedno sample yet this week

90% vs 2%A Monday sample is public in time for Sunday’s outlook 90% of the time; a Thursday sample just 2%. When you sample matters more than lab speed. “+40%” → no effectThe popular “40% more blooms in heatwaves” disappears once you compare the same site in the same month. +82%Inside sheltered shellfish bays, temperature loggers record a median 82% more heatwave days than satellites see. 15.3% vs 3%When mussels are over the toxin limit, oysters are too 15.3% of the time in France, but only 3% in Ireland.

What we can say

  • The weekly outlook ranks the coming fortnight’s bloom weeks better than simply going by the usual pattern for each site and time of year.
  • The signal comes from recent plankton counts at and near each site.
  • Sea temperature, heatwaves, wind, chlorophyll, currents and rivers did not add to it.

Results in the wiki →

What we can’t say (yet)

  • It does not catch more shellfish closures than the Marine Institute’s own rule; its edge is fewer false alarms.
  • So far it has been tested on past seasons. Live scoring starts 25 Oct 2026, with 2027 as the confirmatory season.

FúinnAbout

PA-Marine-Model is a research prototype by Garry Lohan (ATU Galway) and Felix Sproll (Marine Institute), built for Ocean Hackathon Cork 2026. It is not an official Marine Institute product or advice; shellfish safety is decided by official monitoring.

Contains Marine Institute data (open HAB monitoring via ERDDAP), E.U. Copernicus Marine Service information and other open data; this site shows national, regional and yearly aggregates only. Data sources · Credits
